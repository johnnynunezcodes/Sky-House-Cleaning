# Becoming a Contractor

Welcome — we're glad you're considering working with Sky House Cleaning. This guide covers what it means to work with us as an independent contractor, and the practical steps to get set up in California.

## What It Means to Be a Contractor With Us

As an independent contractor, you're running your own cleaning business — Sky House Cleaning is one of the ways you find work, not an employer. In practice, that means:

- You choose which jobs you take on. We'll offer you jobs based on your availability and location, but you're never required to accept one.
- You're responsible for your own taxes. We don't withhold anything from your pay — you'll receive a 1099 at year-end and are responsible for reporting and paying your own income and self-employment taxes.
- You bring your own tools of the trade and cover your own transportation between jobs.
- You're not eligible for employee benefits (health insurance, paid time off, unemployment insurance) through Sky House Cleaning, since you're not an employee.
- You're expected to hold every job to the same standard, laid out in our [Cleaning Standards Manual](Cleaning%20Standards%20Manual.md) and [Service Checklists](Service%20Checklists.md) — that consistency is what our clients count on, no matter who shows up.

## Setting Up Your Business

A few things you'll want in place before your first job:

### 1. Choose a business name

You can operate under your own legal name, or pick a business name for yourself. If you use a name other than your own legal name (like "Bay Area Cleaning Co." instead of your own name), California requires you to file a **Fictitious Business Name (FBN)** statement — sometimes called a "DBA" — with the county clerk where your business is based. Filing is done at the county level, not the state, so requirements and fees vary — check your county clerk's website directly. Most counties also require you to publish the statement in a local newspaper for four consecutive weeks after filing.

### 2. Get an EIN

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS works like a Social Security number for your business. It's free and takes a few minutes at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) — you don't need an accountant or a paid service to get one, and most of the "EIN filing services" you'll find online just charge a fee to do this same free step for you.

### 3. Open a business bank account

Keeping your business income and expenses separate from your personal accounts makes tax time far simpler, and most banks will ask for your EIN (or your name, if you're operating as a sole proprietor without one) to open one.

### 4. Get general liability insurance

This covers you if something in a client's home gets damaged or someone gets hurt while you're working. Costs vary by coverage level and provider, but many independent cleaners in California pay somewhere in the range of $50–$200/month depending on coverage limits — it's worth getting a few quotes and comparing.

### 5. Workers' Compensation

If you're a sole proprietor with no employees of your own, California doesn't require you to carry workers' comp coverage on yourself — there's nothing to file or waive in that case. If you set up as an LLC or corporation and are the sole owner, you can typically request a workers' comp exemption/waiver from your insurance carrier once you have a policy in place, so you're not paying to cover yourself as if you were your own employee. Since the exact process depends on how you've structured your business, it's worth a quick call to an insurance agent to confirm what applies to you.

We're not attorneys or accountants, and this guide is meant as a practical starting point, not legal or tax advice. If you have questions specific to your situation, it's worth a conversation with a licensed professional.
