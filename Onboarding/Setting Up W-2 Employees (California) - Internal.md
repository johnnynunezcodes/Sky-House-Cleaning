

# Setting Up W-2 Employees in California — Internal Guide (Johnny's Eyes Only)

A practical walkthrough for getting Sky House Cleaning set up to legally hire and pay W-2 employees in California. This isn't legal advice — I'm not a lawyer or accountant, and a few of these steps are worth a quick confirmation from one, especially the payroll and insurance setup. But this should get you 90% of the way to knowing what needs to happen and in what order.

## The Honest Recommendation First

Almost everything below — tax registration, payroll tax calculations, new hire reporting, required notices, sick leave tracking — is exactly what a payroll provider (Gusto, ADP RUN, Justworks, Paychex, OnPay, and similar) is built to handle for a flat monthly fee, usually somewhere in the $40–$150/month range for a small team. Given how many moving, deadline-driven pieces there are here (and how the penalties tend to be per-violation, per-employee), most small businesses in your position use one rather than handling payroll taxes and compliance by hand. Worth strongly considering before you dig into doing this manually — a number of them will even walk you through the registration steps below as part of onboarding.

## 1. Get Your Federal EIN

If you don't already have one for Sky House Cleaning, get an Employer Identification Number from the IRS — free, takes a few minutes, immediate issuance at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online).

## 2. Register as an Employer With the EDD

California requires you to register with the Employment Development Department within 15 days of paying more than $100 in wages in a calendar quarter. Register online through [EDD e-Services for Business](https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/) using your Federal EIN and business info — this gets you an 8-digit state Employer Payroll Tax Account Number (SEIN). One EDD account covers all four California payroll taxes:

- **UI (Unemployment Insurance)** — employer-paid, new employers pay 3.4% on the first $7,000 of each employee's wages per year.
- **ETT (Employment Training Tax)** — employer-paid, 0.1%.
- **SDI (State Disability Insurance)** — withheld from the employee's pay, 1.3% with no wage cap.
- **PIT (Personal Income Tax)** — withheld from the employee's pay based on their DE 4.

## 3. Get Workers' Compensation Insurance

Unlike the contractor model, this one isn't optional or something you can waive: **California requires workers' comp coverage the moment you have even one employee**, no exceptions for a small team or part-time hours. Get a policy through an insurance broker or carrier before anyone's first shift — this typically runs a percentage of payroll and varies by class code (cleaning/janitorial is a moderate-risk class), so get a few quotes.

## 4. Report New Hires

Every new employee needs to be reported to the state within 20 calendar days of their start date, using the Report of New Employee(s) (DE 34) through EDD e-Services for Business. Most payroll providers do this automatically the moment you run a new hire through their system.

## 5. Give Required Notices at Hire

A few written notices are legally required at the time of hire, not just "nice to have":

- **Wage Theft Prevention Act Notice (Labor Code 2810.5)** — a written notice to every non-exempt employee covering their pay rate and basis, overtime rates, payday, your business's legal name/address, and workers' comp carrier. Must be updated within 7 days of any change not otherwise reflected on the next pay stub.
- **Paid Sick Leave notice** — see below.
- **"Know Your Rights" notice** — a newer standalone notice (as of Feb 2026) covering paid sick leave, immigration protections, workers' comp, and union rights.
- **I-9 (Employment Eligibility Verification)** — federal, not CA-specific. Completed within 3 business days of the start date, using original identity/work-authorization documents. Keep on file; don't submit anywhere unless requested by an audit.
- **W-4 (federal)** and **DE 4 (California)** — for tax withholding elections.

Most payroll providers bundle these into their new-hire flow automatically.

## 6. Paid Sick Leave

As of 2026, California requires employees to accrue at least 1 hour of paid sick leave per 30 hours worked, with a minimum annual accrual of 48 hours and a mandatory carryover of at least 24 hours (or you can front-load a lump sum instead of accruing). You can require 90 days of employment before it's usable. This applies to every employer with at least one CA employee — no small business exemption.

## 7. Wage & Hour Rules to Build Into Scheduling

These aren't optional and apply to every hourly (non-exempt) employee:

- **Overtime:** 1.5x pay after 8 hours in a day or 40 hours in a week; 2x pay after 12 hours in a day.
- **Meal breaks:** an unpaid, uninterrupted 30-minute meal break required for any shift over 5 hours (a second one after 12 hours). Can only be waived if the shift doesn't exceed 6 hours.
- **Rest breaks:** a paid 10-minute break for every 4 hours worked, and employees must be fully relieved of duty during it.
- **Penalty exposure:** missing a meal or rest break owes the employee an extra hour of pay at their regular rate, per violation, per day — this adds up fast if it's a pattern rather than a one-off.

Worth designing your job scheduling and crew routing with these built in from day one, since a route that's tight enough to skip breaks is a real liability, not just an inconvenience.

## 8. Sexual Harassment Prevention Training

Once you have 5 or more employees (including contractors and temps counted toward that number), California requires sexual harassment prevention training for everyone — 1 hour for non-supervisory employees, 2 hours for anyone who supervises others — within 6 months of hire, and again every 2 years. California's Civil Rights Department offers a free online training that satisfies this at [calcivilrights.ca.gov](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/). Worth setting a calendar reminder once you cross that headcount.

## 9. Required Workplace Postings

Normally these get posted physically where employees can see them during the workday — but since your crews work at client homes rather than reporting to a fixed office, you have two options: post them at wherever functions as your home base/office if employees ever pass through it, or distribute them electronically to every employee (email, a shared employee portal, etc.) if they never do. Free official posters are available from the [CA Department of Industrial Relations](https://www.dir.ca.gov/wpnodb.html).

## 10. Consider (Not Required, But Worth It)

- **Employment Practices Liability Insurance (EPLI)** — covers claims like wrongful termination, harassment, or discrimination. Not legally required, but common for small employers once they have a team.
- **An employee handbook** — not legally required at your size, but useful for setting expectations in writing (attendance, conduct, how PTO/sick leave works, etc.) and helps if a dispute ever comes up.

## Suggested Order of Operations

1. Confirm/get your Federal EIN.
2. Get workers' comp coverage lined up (don't wait until someone's about to start).
3. Pick a payroll provider (or decide to DIY through EDD e-Services directly).
4. Register with EDD.
5. Build your new-hire paperwork packet (I-9, W-4, DE 4, Wage Theft Prevention notice, sick leave notice) — see [New Hire Paperwork & Onboarding Checklist](New%20Hire%20Paperwork%20%26%20Onboarding%20Checklist.md).
6. Set pay rates — see [Employee Pay Structure](Employee%20Pay%20Structure.md).
7. Hire, report the new hire to EDD within 20 days, and start payroll.
