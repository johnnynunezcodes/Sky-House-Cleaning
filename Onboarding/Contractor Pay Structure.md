# Contractor Pay Structure

Pay is for hours actually worked on-site, and it scales with experience and how consistently you meet our standard. These are starting suggestions — adjust as you get a feel for your market and your team.

## Suggested Pay Tiers

| Tier | Who this is | Suggested rate |
|---|---|---|
| New / Provisional | First few jobs, still learning our standard and checklists | $22–$25/hr |
| Experienced | Reliable, efficient, consistently meets the standard with little to no supervision | $27–$33/hr |
| Lead / Top-Tier | Multiple years of experience, can be trusted with our most demanding jobs, can train or lead others, near-zero callbacks | $35–$42/hr |

A new contractor typically starts at the bottom of the New/Provisional range and moves up as they build a track record — most contractors would move to the Experienced tier within their first 30–60 days if they're consistently meeting the standard.

## A Few Things to Keep in Mind

- **Pay is for time worked, not a flat per-job rate.** If a job is split between two contractors, each is paid for their own hours on-site.
- **Bay Area cost of living.** These ranges assume the Bay Area market — adjust if you're paying for work outside the immediate area.
- **Reliability matters as much as speed.** A contractor who's a little slower but never misses a detail or a shift is worth more than one who's fast but inconsistent — factor that into where someone lands in a tier, not just how quickly they finish.
- **Revisit this periodically.** As you get real data on job times and contractor performance, these ranges are worth revisiting rather than treating as fixed.
