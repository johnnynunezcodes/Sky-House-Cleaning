# Employee Pay Structure

Pay for hourly (non-exempt) employees, scaling with experience and how consistently they meet our standard. These are starting suggestions — adjust as you get a feel for your team and market.

## A Wage Floor to Build From

Oakland's local minimum wage is $17.34/hr as of January 1, 2026 (above the CA state minimum of $16.90/hr) — that's the floor for any hours worked within Oakland city limits. If your crews work in other Bay Area cities on a given day, check that city's local minimum too — several (San Francisco, Emeryville, and others) set their own rate above the state minimum, and the applicable rate is wherever the employee is physically working that day, not where your business is based.

## Suggested Pay Tiers

| Tier | Who this is | Suggested rate |
|---|---|---|
| New / Provisional | First few weeks, still learning our standard and checklists | $18–$21/hr |
| Experienced | Reliable, efficient, consistently meets the standard with little to no supervision | $22–$27/hr |
| Lead / Top-Tier | Multiple years of experience, can be trusted with our most demanding jobs, can train or lead others, near-zero callbacks | $29–$34/hr |

A new hire typically starts at the bottom of the New/Provisional range and moves up as they build a track record — most employees would move to the Experienced tier within their first 30–60 days if they're consistently meeting the standard.

## Building Overtime and Breaks Into the Math

As W-2 hourly employees, a few California rules aren't optional and need to be built into scheduling, not just payroll:

- **Overtime:** 1.5x their rate after 8 hours in a day or 40 hours in a week; 2x after 12 hours in a day.
- **Rest breaks:** a paid 10-minute break for every 4 hours worked — this is paid time, not a discount off the hourly total.
- **Meal breaks:** an unpaid 30-minute break required for any shift over 5 hours, and it needs to actually happen, not just be offered.

Practically, this cuts two ways. Overtime is a cost, not a legal risk: if a job runs long enough to trigger it, you simply owe 1.5x (or 2x) pay for those hours. Missed breaks are the real liability: if a route is packed so tight that a rest or meal break can't actually happen, California treats that as a violation and you owe a penalty on top of the pay, an extra hour of wages per missed break, per day. Worth factoring both into how big a home or job list you assign someone in a single day, not just into the pay rate itself.

## A Few Other Things to Keep in Mind

- **Pay is for actual hours worked**, tracked with a real timekeeping method (a time-tracking app, punch clock, or similar) — California is strict about accurate time records for hourly employees.
- **Reliability matters as much as speed.** A steady employee who's a little slower but never misses a detail or a shift is worth more than one who's fast but inconsistent — factor that into tier placement, not just how quickly they finish.
- **Revisit this periodically.** As you get real data on job times and team performance, these ranges are worth revisiting rather than treating as fixed.
