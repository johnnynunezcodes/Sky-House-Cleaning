# Verbal Branding

## Brand Voice

Sky House Cleaning sounds like a trusted, down-to-earth friend who happens to run a spotless, dependable cleaning business. We're warm and personable, never stiff or corporate. We say what we mean clearly and quickly, but we soften it with warmth — direct without being blunt.

### Voice Pillars
- **Warm** — genuinely caring, not just polite. We write like we notice the person behind the request.
- **Down-to-Earth** — plain, honest language. No jargon, no over-the-top luxury talk.
- **Friendly** — approachable and personable, like talking to a neighbor you trust.
- **Clear & To the Point** — short, easy sentences. We respect people's time.
- **Never Sharp** — even when we're being direct (confirmations, policies, pricing), we keep it soft-edged. No cold, transactional, or curt phrasing.

## How We Want Customers to Feel
- At ease — one less thing to worry about
- Genuinely cared for — not just "serviced"
- Confident — they know exactly what they're getting and can trust us
- Comfortable — never talked down to or oversold to

## Point of View
- Use "we/us" — Sky House speaks in the first person. ("We'll take care of your kitchen," not "Sky House Cleaning will take care of your kitchen.")
- Address the customer directly as "you" — keep the focus on their home and their experience.

## Tone Guidelines
- Contractions are welcome (we'll, you're, don't) — keeps things conversational.
- Keep sentences short to medium. Break long explanations into simple statements.
- Prefer active voice over passive voice.
- It's fine to be brief — just soften direct statements with a warm word or two rather than leaving them blunt.
  - Too sharp: "Cancel 24 hours in advance or you'll be charged."
  - Better: "We just ask for 24 hours' notice if you need to cancel, so we can keep things running smoothly for everyone."
- Avoid exclamation points except when genuinely earned. Enthusiasm should come through word choice, not punctuation.
- No ALL CAPS for emphasis.

## Words & Phrases to Embrace
fresh, cared for, comfortable, reliable, trusted, welcome, breathe easy, taken care of, home

## Words & Phrases to Avoid
- Corporate jargon: leverage, optimize, solutions, streamline, best-in-class, world-class
- Overly clinical language: sanitize, eliminate germs, disinfect (unless factually necessary) — prefer clean, fresh, spotless
- Discount/bargain language: cheap, budget, deal, bargain — we're premium, not a bargain service
- Overly upscale/pretentious language: luxury, opulent, exclusive, elite — this breaks the down-to-earth feel
- "Maid" or "maid service" — prefer cleaner, cleaning professional, or our team
- Hype/salesy language: amazing, incredible, game-changing, revolutionary
- Cold or transactional phrasing with no warmth

## Quick Gut-Check
Before publishing anything: does this sound like a real, caring person said it — or like a corporate policy page? If it leans corporate, cut the jargon and soften the edges.
